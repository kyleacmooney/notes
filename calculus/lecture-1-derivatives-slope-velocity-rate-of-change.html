Lecture 1 Derivatives Slope Velocity Rate Of Change

Introduction to the derivative from the geometric perspective — defining it as the slope of a tangent line, computing it via the limit of a difference quotient, and applying it to compute derivatives of 1/x and x^n (the power rule).

1. The Tangent Line Problem

¶ Motivation

The central question of differentiation is: given a curve y = f(x) and a point P on it, what is the slope of the tangent line at P? This geometric interpretation is the first of three perspectives on the derivative — the others being rate of change (physics) and its role in measurement (science broadly).

:= Tangent Line at a Point
$$\text{Given } y = f(x) \text{ and a point } P = (x_0,\, f(x_0)), \text{ the tangent line at } P \text{ has equation:} \\ y - f(x_0) = m(x - x_0) \quad \text{where } m = f'(x_0).$$
Finding the point is algebra; finding the slope m is the calculus problem.
:= The Derivative (Geometric Definition)
$$f'(x_0) = \text{the slope of the tangent line to } y = f(x) \text{ at the point } (x_0,\, f(x_0)).$$
This is the geometric interpretation of the derivative.

2. The Difference Quotient

¶ Key Insight

We cannot compute the slope of a tangent line directly from one point — slope requires two points. The key idea is to use a nearby second point Q on the curve, compute the slope of the secant line PQ, and take the limit as Q approaches P.

:= Setup for the Difference Quotient
$$\text{Let } P = (x_0,\, f(x_0)) \text{ and } Q = (x_0 + \Delta x,\, f(x_0 + \Delta x)). \\ \text{Rise: } \Delta f = f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0) \qquad \text{Run: } \Delta x \\ \text{Slope of secant } PQ = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}$$
Both numerator and denominator are differences — hence 'difference quotient.'
:= The Derivative (Limit Definition)
$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0)}{\Delta x}$$
This is the formal definition. The tangent line is the limit of secant lines as Q → P.
✎ The Algebraic Challenge
$$\text{At } \Delta x = 0, \text{ the difference quotient is } \tfrac{0}{0}. \text{ The work of computing a derivative} \\ \text{is always: simplify until you can cancel the } \Delta x \text{ that makes the denominator zero.}$$
Every derivative computation follows this same pattern: write, simplify, cancel, take limit.

3. Example: Derivative of 1/x

= The Function
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$$
→ Step 1: Write the Difference Quotient
$$\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{x_0 + \Delta x} - \dfrac{1}{x_0}}{\Delta x}$$
Plug the function into the limit definition.
→ Step 2: Common Denominator
$$= \frac{1}{\Delta x} \cdot \frac{x_0 - (x_0 + \Delta x)}{(x_0 + \Delta x)\, x_0} = \frac{1}{\Delta x} \cdot \frac{-\Delta x}{(x_0 + \Delta x)\, x_0}$$
Combine the fractions in the numerator to reveal the cancellable Δx.
→ Step 3: Cancel Δx
$$= \frac{-1}{(x_0 + \Delta x)\, x_0}$$
This is the crucial algebraic step — after cancellation, setting Δx = 0 no longer gives 0/0.
→ Step 4: Take the Limit
$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{-1}{(x_0 + \Delta x)\, x_0} = \frac{-1}{x_0^2}$$
∴ Derivative of 1/x
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = -\frac{1}{x^2}$$
Negative (hyperbola slopes down) and approaches 0 as x → ∞ (curve flattens). Both match geometric intuition.

4. Application: Triangle Under the Tangent to y = 1/x

¶ Setup

Consider the triangle formed by the coordinate axes and the tangent line to y = 1/x at a point (x₀, y₀) where y₀ = 1/x₀. We use the derivative we just computed to find its area.

→ Tangent Line Equation
$$y - y_0 = -\frac{1}{x_0^2}(x - x_0) \quad \text{where } y_0 = \frac{1}{x_0}$$
This is the only step that requires calculus — everything else is algebra/geometry.
→ X-Intercept
$$\text{Set } y = 0: \quad -\frac{1}{x_0} = -\frac{1}{x_0^2}(x - x_0) \implies x = 2x_0$$
→ Y-Intercept
$$\text{By symmetry of } xy = 1 \text{ under } x \leftrightarrow y: \quad y\text{-intercept} = 2y_0 = \frac{2}{x_0}$$
The curve xy = 1 is symmetric about the line y = x, so swapping x and y gives the same curve.
∴ Area of the Triangle
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(2x_0)(2y_0) = 2\, x_0 y_0 = 2 \cdot 1 = 2$$
Remarkably, the area is always 2, independent of which point on the hyperbola is chosen.

5. Notation for the Derivative

✎ Equivalent Notations
$$f'(x_0) \quad = \quad \frac{df}{dx}\bigg|_{x=x_0} \quad = \quad \frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x=x_0} \quad = \quad \frac{d}{dx} f(x)\bigg|_{x=x_0}$$
Newton's f′ is compact; Leibniz's df/dx reminds you of the difference quotient Δf/Δx; d/dx emphasizes differentiation as an operator.

6. The Power Rule

★ Power Rule
$$\text{For } n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots: \quad \frac{d}{dx}\, x^n = n\, x^{n-1}$$
One of the most frequently used differentiation rules — derived here from the Binomial Theorem.
→ Step 1: Difference Quotient
$$\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \frac{(x + \Delta x)^n - x^n}{\Delta x}$$
→ Step 2: Binomial Expansion
$$(x + \Delta x)^n = x^n + n\, x^{n-1}\, \Delta x + \binom{n}{2} x^{n-2}(\Delta x)^2 + \cdots$$
Only the first two terms matter — all remaining terms contain (Δx)² or higher powers.
→ Step 3: Substitute and Cancel
$$\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \frac{n\, x^{n-1}\, \Delta x + O((\Delta x)^2)}{\Delta x} = n\, x^{n-1} + O(\Delta x)$$
The x^n terms cancel in the numerator; then Δx cancels from numerator and denominator.
→ Step 4: Take the Limit
$$\frac{d}{dx}\, x^n = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \bigl[n\, x^{n-1} + O(\Delta x)\bigr] = n\, x^{n-1}$$
◇ Differentiating Polynomials
$$\text{Differentiate term by term: } \quad \frac{d}{dx}(x^3 + 5x^{10}) = 3x^2 + 50x^9$$
The derivative of a sum is the sum of derivatives, and constants factor out — so the power rule handles all polynomials.

Key Takeaways

✎ Summary
$$\begin{aligned} &1.\; f'(x_0) = \text{slope of tangent line to } y = f(x) \text{ at } x_0 \\[4pt] &2.\; f'(x_0) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0)}{\Delta x} \\[4pt] &3.\; \text{The algebraic work: simplify until you can cancel the } \Delta x \text{ causing } \tfrac{0}{0} \\[4pt] &4.\; \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = -\frac{1}{x^2} \qquad 5.\; \frac{d}{dx}\, x^n = n\, x^{n-1} \end{aligned}$$
The geometric interpretation and limit definition are the conceptual core; the power rule is the first computational tool.
