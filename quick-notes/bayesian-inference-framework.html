Bayesian Inference Framework

A complete mathematical framework for updating beliefs rationally as new evidence arrives, with applications to estimation, hypothesis testing, and decision-making under uncertainty.

The Foundation

Probability represents degrees of belief, not just frequencies. We update beliefs as we gather evidence. Bayes' theorem provides the mathematical machinery for this updating process.

★ Bayes' Theorem
$$P(H|E) = \frac{P(E|H) \cdot P(H)}{P(E)}$$
The fundamental equation relating prior beliefs to posterior beliefs after observing evidence.
:= Component Terms
$$\begin{aligned} P(H) &= \text{prior — your belief before seeing evidence} \\ P(E|H) &= \text{likelihood — how probable is the evidence if } H \text{ is true} \\ P(E) &= \text{marginal likelihood — total probability of the evidence} \\ P(H|E) &= \text{posterior — your updated belief after seeing evidence} \end{aligned}$$
Each term plays a distinct role in the belief-updating process.

Computing the Marginal Likelihood

The denominator P(E) requires summing over all possible hypotheses, which normalizes the posterior to a valid probability distribution.

= Discrete Case
$$P(E) = \sum_{i} P(E|H_i) \cdot P(H_i)$$
Sum over all mutually exclusive hypotheses.
= Continuous Case
$$P(E) = \int P(E|\theta) \cdot P(\theta) \, d\theta$$
Integrate over the parameter space when hypotheses form a continuum.

Alternative Formulations

= Odds Form
$$\underbrace{\frac{P(H|E)}{P(\neg H|E)}}_{\text{posterior odds}} = \underbrace{\frac{P(E|H)}{P(E|\neg H)}}_{\text{likelihood ratio}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{P(H)}{P(\neg H)}}_{\text{prior odds}}$$
Often more intuitive—evidence multiplies your odds by the likelihood ratio.

Sequential Updating

When you see multiple pieces of evidence, yesterday's posterior becomes today's prior. This captures how rational agents accumulate knowledge over time.

= General Sequential Update
$$P(H|E_1, E_2) = \frac{P(E_2|H, E_1) \cdot P(H|E_1)}{P(E_2|E_1)}$$
Each new observation updates the current belief state.
= Conditionally Independent Evidence
$$P(H|E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n) \propto P(H) \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(E_i|H)$$
When evidence is independent given H, likelihoods simply multiply.

Example: Medical Testing

A classic demonstration of why priors matter enormously. Even with a highly accurate test, rare diseases remain unlikely after a positive result.

⊢ Given Information
$$\begin{aligned} P(\text{disease}) &= 0.01 \quad \text{(1\% base rate)} \\ P(\text{positive}|\text{disease}) &= 0.95 \quad \text{(sensitivity)} \\ P(\text{positive}|\text{no disease}) &= 0.05 \quad \text{(false positive rate)} \end{aligned}$$
Typical parameters for a medical screening test.
→ Apply Bayes' Theorem
$$P(\text{disease}|\text{positive}) = \frac{0.95 \times 0.01}{0.95 \times 0.01 + 0.05 \times 0.99}$$
Substitute values and expand the denominator.
∴ Result
$$= \frac{0.0095}{0.0095 + 0.0495} = \frac{0.0095}{0.059} \approx 0.16$$
Only 16% chance of disease despite positive test—the low prior dominates.

Bayesian vs Frequentist Paradigms

= Frequentist View
$$P(\text{data}|\theta) \quad \text{— } \theta \text{ is fixed, data is random}$$
Parameters are fixed unknowns; probability describes sampling variability.
= Bayesian View
$$P(\theta|\text{data}) \quad \text{— } \theta \text{ is random, we condition on observed data}$$
Parameters have probability distributions representing uncertainty.

Point Estimation

= MAP Estimation
$$\hat{\theta}_{MAP} = \arg\max_{\theta} P(\theta|D) = \arg\max_{\theta} \left[ \log P(D|\theta) + \log P(\theta) \right]$$
Find the most probable parameter value given the data and prior.
= MLE (Frequentist)
$$\hat{\theta}_{MLE} = \arg\max_{\theta} P(D|\theta)$$
Without the prior term—the prior acts as a regularizer in MAP.

Conjugate Priors

When prior and posterior belong to the same distributional family, the mathematics becomes elegant and closed-form updates are possible.

= Beta-Binomial Conjugacy
$$\text{Beta prior} + \text{Binomial likelihood} \to \text{Beta posterior}$$
The Beta distribution is conjugate to the Binomial likelihood.
= Update Rule
$$P(\theta) = \text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta) \implies P(\theta|k \text{ successes}, n \text{ trials}) = \text{Beta}(\alpha + k, \beta + n - k)$$
Simply add successes to α and failures to β.

Philosophical Principles

★ Evidence Criterion
$$P(H|E) > P(H) \iff P(E|H) > P(E)$$
Evidence supports H if and only if H makes that evidence more likely than average.
⊢ Cromwell's Rule
$$0 < P(H) < 1 \quad \text{for any } H \text{ not logically certain}$$
Never assign probability 0 or 1 to empirical claims—no evidence can update away from certainty.
